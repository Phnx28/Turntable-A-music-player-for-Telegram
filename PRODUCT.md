# Product

## Register

product

## Users

A single owner using a self-hosted player to browse and listen to audio already stored in Telegram. They may be casually exploring a large personal collection, quickly resuming a track, or curating sources, likes, and queues from a desktop browser. The interface should support focused listening without making the user manage Telegram's underlying complexity.

## Product Purpose

Telegram Turntable indexes music from selected Telegram chats, channels, bots, private conversations, and Saved Messages, then turns that collection into a browsable library with on-demand playback. It succeeds when the listener can find a track quickly, understand where it came from, start playback confidently, and keep listening with minimal friction.

## Brand Personality

Bold, chic, focused. The product should feel like a considered personal listening instrument: confident and expressive, but never noisy or ornamental. Copy should be concise, assured, and human.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic streaming-service sameness, dashboard-like clutter, playful consumer-app decoration, neon music-player clichés, and opaque Telegram utility chrome. Do not make the interface feel like an admin panel, a social feed, or a marketing landing page. Avoid visual novelty that competes with the track, artwork, or listening flow.

## Design Principles

- **Make listening the center of gravity.** Finding, starting, controlling, and resuming playback should remain the clearest path through every screen.
- **Turn collection complexity into calm structure.** Sources, metadata, queue state, and sync status should be legible without exposing implementation detail too early.
- **Be confidently editorial.** Use strong hierarchy, deliberate density, and a distinctive typographic voice instead of decorative effects or generic component patterns.
- **Respect provenance.** Make the Telegram source and track context available so the library feels trustworthy and personally owned.
- **Keep power close, not loud.** Search, sorting, queue actions, metadata editing, and sharing should be discoverable and efficient without overwhelming the primary listening surface.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for the interface. Preserve keyboard access and visible focus states, provide accessible names and state announcements for playback and async sync actions, maintain usable contrast in both themes, keep controls at touch-friendly sizes on coarse pointers, support reduced motion, and avoid communicating status through color alone. Dynamic titles, source names, metadata, and lyrics must remain usable when they are long or contain non-Latin scripts.
